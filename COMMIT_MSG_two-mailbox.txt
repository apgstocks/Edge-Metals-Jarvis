TWO THINGS BREAK SILENTLY WHEN A SECOND MAILBOX APPEARS:

1. THE SAME EMAIL, TWICE. A Gmail message id is per-mailbox. One mail to
   both bose@ and apsara@ has two ids, so `seen` cannot tell they are the
   same — she would read one request twice in one digest under two
   numbers, having paid for two Gemini calls. Deduped on the RFC 5322
   Message-ID, stored in the SAME `seen` map under a `mid:` prefix rather
   than a new store field: a new field is a seventh chance for the
   allowlist to eat something, and this way trimSeen already prunes it.
   No Message-ID falls back to the old behaviour — a duplicate shown
   twice beats an email suppressed because two mails looked identical.

2. BOSE'S REPLIES BECOMING HERS. The has-she-replied test was
   lastFrom.includes(me) where `me` is the MAILBOX OWNER. In bose@'s
   inbox that makes Bose answering a customer read as SHE REPLIED — and
   senderHistoryLine turns `replied` into "she reliably answers this
   sender — an active working relationship", which is a claim about
   Apsara fed into every future prompt. Her prior would have been built
   out of Bose's behaviour, at volume, from the moment bose@ was read.
   Now: `replied` means Apsara, `team_replied` means anyone else at Edge
   Metals, and the history line says the team handles this sender
   without pretending she does — and without implying the mail needs
   nobody, since somebody is doing that work.
